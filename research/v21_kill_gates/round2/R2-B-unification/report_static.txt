3. HEAVY-VECTOR MS-bar MATCHING CONSTANT (item b)
-------------------------------------------------
Result used (sign fixed by derivation; labels below):  at mu = M_V,
     alpha_i^-1(low, MS-bar) = alpha_G^-1(high, MS-bar) - [C2(G) - C2(H_i)]/(12 pi) = alpha_G^-1 - S_V,i/(12 pi),
with S_V,i = sum over the heavy (eaten) vectors of the factor-i Dynkin index = (5, 6, 6, 8) for (SU3, SU2L, SU2R, B-L)
at M_U [EXACT from the (3,2,2)_{-2/3} + (3,1,1)_{4/3} vectors and their conjugates], and 6/5 for U(1)_Y (GUT norm.)
from W_R^+- at v_R [EXACT].  In the code's convention alpha_high = alpha_low + (1/2pi) sum b^k ln(M_k/mu) + S_V/(12 pi).
Derivation [EXACT given two literature facts]: (i) in DR-bar the one-loop matching has no constant for any spin
(Langacker-Polonsky hep-ph/9210235, LIT-abstract/snippet; Antoniadis-Kounnas-Tamvakis 1982, LIT-abstract);
(ii) for an unbroken group 1/alpha_DR = 1/alpha_MS - C2(G)/(12 pi) (alpha_s^DR = alpha_s^MS (1 + alpha_s/4pi) for SU(3);
Martin-Vaughn hep-ph/9308222 computes this scheme difference, LIT-abstract; coefficient quoted from standard knowledge,
NOT re-read in this session).  Then alpha_i,MS^-1 = alpha_i,DR^-1 + C_i/(12pi) = alpha_G,DR^-1 + C_i/(12pi) =
alpha_G,MS^-1 - (C_G - C_i)/(12pi).  This is the Weinberg-Hall structure lambda_i ~ (C_G - C_i) - 21 Tr(t_V^2 ln(M_V/mu))
in which the constant and the vector log enter with opposite relative signs (the round-1 feasibility skeptic's form
-(2/21)(b_V+b_GB) + 2(b_V+b_GB) ln(M_V/mu) has the same structure: its "+1" sign).  The round-1 physics skeptic's claim
"MS-bar has no constant, a DR-bar-like constant would be the ambiguity" is the reverse of (i)+(ii) and is not adopted.
Primary papers (Weinberg PLB 91 (1980) 51, Hall NPB 178 (1981) 75, Bertolini-Di Luzio-Malinsky 0912.1796) could not
be read (no WebFetch; the searches returned no text of them): the sign is therefore EXACT-conditional on (i), (ii).
Size: shifts (0.133, 0.159, 0.159, 0.212) in alpha^-1 at M_U and 0.032 in alpha_1^-1 at v_R; effect on the solutions
(two loops): M_U +4.5-4.6% (all four contents); tau_p +20.2-20.4%; v_R +4.8-5.0%; Lambda_2L +8.3-8.5%.  All main
numbers below use the derived sign; the opposite sign and 'off' are tabulated as systematics.

9. PROTON DECAY NORMALISATION (item d)
--------------------------------------
Repository normalisation (instructed; proton_decay_falsification_gate_v20.gauge_lifetime_years):
  tau = (4/pi)(f_pi^2/m_p) M_X^4 alpha_U^-2 / (alpha_H^2 A_R^2 F_q), f_pi = 0.139, alpha_H = 0.012 GeV^3, A_R = 2.726,
  F_q = 7.6 = 2(1+|V_ud|^2)^2, M_X = M_U = M(3,2,2).  The literature form behind it, Gamma = (pi m_p/4 f_pi^2) alpha_U^2
  F_q A_R^2 alpha_H^2 / M_X^4, is confirmed at snippet level (LIT-abstract: e.g. 2306.02401, 1003.2625 where the width
  is m_p/(64 pi f_pi^2) (1+D+F)^2 alpha_bar_H^2 (g^2 A_R/M_X^2)^2, i.e. alpha_H = (1+D+F) alpha_bar_H).
Second normalisation (EST, derived here; r2b_model.proton_lifetime_direct_years): direct lattice form factor
  W0 = <pi0|(ud)_R u_L|p> ~ 0.131 GeV^2 (Aoki-Izubuchi-Shintani-Soni 1705.01338; the abstract read says the direct form
  factors are 1.4x smaller than the indirect BChPT ones; the value 0.131 is quoted from memory, LIT-unverified),
  SO(10) X,Y,X',Y' exchange with k^2 = g_U^2/(2 M_X^2), Weyl Fierz (factor 2): C^{LR} = 2k^2(1+|V_ud|^2), C^{RL} = 4k^2,
  Gamma = (m_p/32pi)(1 - m_pi^2/m_p^2)^2 A_R^2 W0^2 (|C^{LR}|^2 + |C^{RL}|^2).
  => tau_direct = tau_repo / 4.53 for every (M_X, alpha_U) [NUM].  The repository formula corresponds to an effective
  W_eff = alpha_H/(sqrt2 f_pi) = 0.061 GeV^2, i.e. BChPT WITHOUT the (1+D+F) = 2.27 factor; with it, W = 0.139 GeV^2,
  consistent with the direct lattice value.  Independent literature check: the MEMPHYS study (hep-ex/0607026,
  LIT-snippet) quotes tau(p -> e+ pi0) ~ 1e35 yr (M_X/1e16 GeV)^4 (alpha_U^-1/25)^2 with newer lattice input; at
  (1e16 GeV, 25) the direct normalisation gives 9.3e34 yr and the repository one 4.2e35 yr.  Hence the repository
  normalisation is probably optimistic by a factor ~4-5 (EST); both are reported and the criterion is evaluated in both.
  Super-K 90% CL bound tau > 2.4e34 yr (2010.16098, repository citation) requires M_X > 4.16e15 GeV (repo) /
  6.07e15 GeV (direct) at alpha_U^-1 = 34.5.  Hyper-K 90% CL sensitivity 7.8e34 yr (1805.04163 via 2002.04890,
  LIT-snippet; ~1-2e35 for longer exposure, LIT-snippet).

13. WHY THE REALISABLE CONTENTS FAIL: ONE-LOOP ANALYTIC STRUCTURE [EXACT arithmetic, r2b_analytic.json]
---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
With L1 = ln(v_R/M_Z), L2 = ln(M_U/v_R) and tree matching,
   2 pi (alpha_1^-1 - alpha_2^-1)(M_Z) = 185.0 = (109/15) L1 + A L2,   A = 3/5 b_2R + 2/5 b_BL - b_2L,
   2 pi (alpha_2^-1 - alpha_3^-1)(M_Z) = 132.5 = (23/6) L1 + B L2,     B = b_2L - b_3      (G3221 segment),
so L2 = 34.90/(B - 0.5275 A), ln(M_U/M_Z) = 25.46 + L2 (1 - 0.1376 A).  The two-loop M_U is 0.43-0.49 x the
one-loop M_U for these contents, so tau_p > 2.4e34 (repo normalisation) needs M_U(1L) >~ 9e15, and v_R(2L) >= 1e11
needs A L2 <~ 40.  Together: B <~ 5.16 - 0.18 A with A <~ 3.3.
   content   A       B      1L v_R    1L M_U      verdict of the inequality
   C0        17/15   31/6   3.2e12    6.6e15      B too large by ~0.21
   C1        1       11/2   4.0e12    4.4e15      B too large by ~0.52
   C2        11/3    13/3   6.8e9     1.41e16     tau fine, A L2 = 53 > 40 (v_R too low)
   C1'       10/3    14/3   4.3e10    6.9e15      marginal on both
   T2        -4/15   25/6   1.4e13    4.6e16      (round 1; not realisable)
Per-multiplet (dA, dB): (8,3,1)_0 (-8/3, +7/6) is what drives C0/C1 to large B (low M_U); a bidoublet adds (-2/15,
+1/3); (1,3,1)_0 (-1/3, +1/3); (8,1,3)_0 (+8/5, -3/2); (6,2,2)_{2/3} (-4/15, -4/3); (8,2,2)_0 (-16/15, -4/3);
(8,1,1)_0 (0, -1/2); colour triplets (3,1,1) (+1/15, -1/6).  Hence a light (8,3,1)_0 without an SU(3)-heavy partner
lowers M_U below the Super-K limit, and removing it (C2) drives v_R to ~1e10: this is the structural reason.

8b. U(1)_X OF B63 [EXACT coefficients, NUM running]
-----------------------------------------------------
Integer-X normalisation (X(16_F) = 1): below M_Phi b_X = 254041/3, b_{10,X} = 27044, b_{X,10} = 1216980,
b_XX = 388484332; full content (above M_Phi) b_X = 101203 (= K0b), b_{10,X} = 33240, b_{X,10} = 1495800,
b_XX = 439863564.  With the two-loop self term included, alpha_X(M_Pl,red) <= 1 requires alpha_X^-1(M_U) >= 9.5e4-1.06e5
(g_X(M_U) <= 0.0109-0.0115; K0b's one-loop 0.013); the stricter EST criterion alpha_X X_max^2 <= 1 at M_Pl,red
(X_max = 63) requires alpha_X^-1(M_Pl) >= 3969 and gives practically the same g_X(M_U) bound (0.0107-0.0113), because
b_X ~ 1e5 makes alpha_X^-1 fall by ~1.6e4 per e-fold: in every admissible case the U(1)_X reaches alpha_X = 1 within
~0.2-0.3 e-folds above M_Pl,red (1.2-1.3 M_Pl,red at alpha_X^-1(M_Pl) = 3969).  Feedback on SO(10): with the worst
admissible g_X (alpha_X(M_Pl) = 1) alpha_10^-1(M_Pl,red) drops by only 0.16-0.20 (e.g. C2 2L: 17.96 -> 17.80; C0 3L:
9.49 -> 9.28), i.e. U(1)_X is not the binding constraint for B63 (unlike K1's F17 estimate, where the feedback was
larger because b_{10,X}/b_X was larger and alpha_10^-1(M_Pl) smaller).  g_X is a free parameter; the bound is a
contract constraint (g_X(M_U) <~ 0.011), not a kill.
